Accelerated Life Testing and Warranty Optimisation for LED Systems

You cannot wait ten years to learn how long an LED lasts. You can stress it and infer.

Type Individual projectInstitution CentraleSupélecDate December 2025 – January 2026Field Reliability engineering
WeibullArrheniusAccelerated Life TestingWarranty Pricing
§ 1

The problem

Products designed to last years cannot be tested for years. Accelerated life testing raises temperature and current beyond normal operating conditions to induce failures quickly, then extrapolates back to expected life under normal use.

Two difficulties follow. Many units survive the test, so the failure data is right-censored and cannot be treated as a complete sample. And the extrapolation depends entirely on the assumed relationship between stress and life, which must be estimated rather than assumed.

§ 2

Why it matters

The reliability estimate is not the end of the analysis. A warranty is a financial position taken on that estimate: a longer warranty is worth more to the customer, and costs more in expected claims. The two questions are usually answered by different departments, which is how firms end up offering warranties they cannot price.

§ 3

Model

Failure times were modelled with right-censored Weibull distributions under each stress condition. Arrhenius and multi-stress accelerated life test models then related the fitted parameters to temperature and current, allowing lifetime prediction at normal operating conditions.

From that distribution the standard reliability metrics follow: mean time to failure, median life, B10 life, and the probability of failure within any candidate warranty period. Those probabilities feed a warranty and pricing optimisation model incorporating demand sensitivity to warranty length.

§ 4

Scale and implementation

Accelerated lifetime and degradation data across multiple temperature and current stress conditions, with censoring handled explicitly in the likelihood rather than by discarding survivors.

§ 5

Results

The optimisation identifies a premium pricing strategy attached to a long warranty term, profitable precisely because the reliability analysis shows the warranty risk at that term is low. The long warranty is a marketing asset whose cost the reliability model has bounded.

§ 6

Summary

The project connects a statistical estimate to a commercial decision. The warranty term is not chosen because it sounds generous but because the fitted failure distribution says what it will cost.
