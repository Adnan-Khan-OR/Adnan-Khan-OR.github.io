01 · The problem

One decision now. Many possible operating futures.

A planner commits to expensive generation assets today, then adapts dispatch when uncertain demand is revealed.

What generation mix minimizes investment, production and shortage costs while remaining robust to uncertain electricity demand?

Stage 1 · Before uncertainty

Install capacity

Choose coal, gas, nuclear and oil capacity, subject to at least 15 MW in total.

→
Uncertainty

Demand is observed

A scenario reveals the base, medium and peak load-block requirements.

→
Stage 2 · Recourse

Dispatch or shed load

Allocate generation across demand blocks; shortage remains feasible but expensive.

03 · Model and method

Direct solution, decomposition and tail-risk control.

Three complementary methods expose both the optimal decision and the algorithmic structure behind it.

01

SAA extensive form

Replace the expected recourse cost with the average across sampled demand scenarios and solve the full deterministic equivalent.

02

Multi-cut Benders

Separate the capacity master from one operating subproblem per scenario. Use dual subgradients to tighten scenario-level recourse approximations.

03

CVaR formulation

Replace average recourse cost with tail conditional cost at confidence level alpha to quantify the price of protecting against adverse outcomes.

min investment(x) + eta + [1 / ((1 - alpha)N)] Σ s_k
s_k ≥ Q_k(x) - eta

04 · Results

A reproducible risk-neutral benchmark.

The 500-scenario extensive form and Benders decomposition converge to effectively the same objective.

Expected cost on 2,000 fresh out-of-sample scenarios.

Risk-neutral capacity: coal 3.508, gas 1.652, nuclear 5.538 and oil 4.302 MW.

The lower and upper bounds meet at iteration 11; the final extensive-form difference is 0.000325 EUR/MWh.

Sample-size diagnostic. N=500 was selected as the largest tested, computationally tractable sample; repeated-seed confidence intervals are a recommended extension.

Validation insight: the 2,000-scenario out-of-sample result is within about 0.25% of the 500-scenario in-sample objective, supporting the practical quality of the selected solution.

06 · Cleaned implementation

Code organized around decisions and verification.

The refactored module separates sampling, extensive forms, recourse evaluation, decomposition, CVaR evaluation and tests.

power_planning.py · corrected CVaR multi-cut
for scenario_index in range(sample_size):
    master_constraints.append(
        excess[scenario_index]
        >= recourse_values[scenario_index]
        + subgradients[scenario_index] @ (capacity - capacity_hat)
        - eta
    )
Why this matters: keeping -eta in the cut links each scenario's excess loss to the master VaR decision. Automated tests now verify that CVaR-Benders matches the extensive form on the same scenarios.

07 · My explanation

How I would explain the project in an interview.

“I treated generation planning as a sequence of decisions. Capacity must be purchased before demand is known, while dispatch can adapt afterward. I first solved the sampled model directly, then reproduced it with Benders decomposition. Finally, I used CVaR to show how the technology mix changes when tail losses matter more than average cost.”

Strength: extensive-form and Benders objectives are independently cross-validated.
Limitation: demand blocks are assumed independent and represented by fitted distributions.
Next step: use repeated SAA replications, confidence intervals and correlated demand.
Model scope: the implementation follows the assignment's aggregate capacity convention exactly.
